Brewing the perfect cup at home

By the Brewly team, 12 September 2026

At Brewly we believe great coffee should be simple. Our team has tested hundreds of brewers in the Brewly kitchen, and the one we come back to every morning is the humble cafetière. It needs no paper filters, no power and very little practice, which is why it is the first brewer we recommend to new Brewly customers.

What you need

  • A cafetière (any size; we use the 8-cup Brewly Classic)
  • Freshly roasted coffee beans
  • A burr grinder set to a coarse grind
  • A kettle and filtered water

Step by step

Warm the plunger pot with a splash of hot water, then tip it away. Grind 30 grams of coffee beans coarsely, about the texture of sea salt. A fine grind slips through the mesh and makes the cup muddy, so a burr grinder that gives an even, coarse grind makes a real difference.

Add the coffee, pour in 500 ml of water just off the boil and stir gently. Put the lid on with the plunger raised and wait four minutes. Then press the plunger down slowly and steadily. If it is hard to push, the grind is too fine.

Serve straight away. Coffee left sitting on the grounds keeps extracting and turns bitter, so pour any extra into a warmed jug.

Cleaning your press pot

Scoop the grounds into your compost, rinse the beaker and take the mesh filter apart once a week to wash it properly. Old coffee oils trapped in the mesh are the most common reason a cafetière starts to taste stale.

Cafetière or French press?

They are the same thing. In the UK most people say cafetière, while in the US it is usually called a French press.
