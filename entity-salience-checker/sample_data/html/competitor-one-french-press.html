How to use a French press

A French press is one of the easiest ways to make full-bodied coffee at home. The French press steeps ground coffee in hot water, then a metal mesh plunger separates the grounds from the brew. Because the French press uses a metal filter instead of paper, more of the coffee's oils end up in your cup.

What you need for a French press

You need a French press, coffee beans, a grinder that can produce a coarse grind, a kettle and a timer. Any French press works; Bodum makes one of the best-known designs, but the method is the same for every brand.

French press ratio and grind

Start with a ratio of 1:15, for example 30 grams of coffee to 450 grams of water. Use a coarse grind. A burr grinder gives you even particles, which matters more in a French press than in most brewers because fine particles pass through the mesh.

How to brew with a French press, step by step

  1. Preheat the French press with hot water and discard it.
  2. Add the ground coffee and pour in water just off the boil.
  3. Stir, put the lid on with the plunger up, and steep for four minutes.
  4. Press the plunger down slowly and pour immediately.

Common French press mistakes

Grinding too fine makes the plunger hard to push and the coffee muddy. Leaving coffee in the French press after brewing makes it bitter, so decant it. If your French press coffee tastes flat, clean the mesh filter: old coffee oils go stale quickly.

The French press is also called a cafetière in the UK and a press pot in some cafés, but the brewing method is identical.
